About the Institute

Independent inquiry into technology and public life.

The Institute studies technological change as an economic, institutional, political, scientific, and social process.

Purpose

Advance knowledge and improve public decision-making.

The Technology & Society Institute examines the economics of innovation, technological change, and the policies and regulations that govern emerging technologies. Its mission is to advance knowledge and inform policymakers through independent, rigorous research, educational programs, policy dialogue, and conferences addressing major technological and societal challenges.

The Institute connects economics, public policy, political economy, technology and society studies, security studies, law, engineering, and computational research. This interdisciplinary approach clarifies how technologies develop, how institutions govern them, and how their benefits, risks, and authority consequences are distributed.

Analytical perspective

Mutual shaping and institutional evolution.

Scientific knowledge enables new capabilities, while technological instruments transform how scientists conduct research. Technologies reorganize markets, institutions, military power, social relationships, and everyday life. Public values, political conflict, economic incentives, regulation, and organizational practice simultaneously determine which technologies receive investment, how they are designed, and whose purposes they serve.

The Institute studies this reciprocal process across time. Research traces how technological systems emerge, acquire authority, encounter public contestation, and change through regulatory review, institutional adaptation, and social use.

Institutional principles

01

Research independence

Questions and conclusions follow evidence and analytical judgment.

02

Methodological rigor

Methods fit the problem, disclose limitations, and withstand informed scrutiny.

03

Interdisciplinary inquiry

Technological change requires economic, political, legal, social, and technical analysis.

04

Public relevance

Research should clarify policy choices, consequences, and institutional responsibilities.
