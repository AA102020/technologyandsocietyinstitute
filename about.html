About the Institute

Independent inquiry into technology and public life.

The Institute studies technological change as a process shaped through institutions and social practice, with consequences for economic organization and public authority.

Purpose

Advance knowledge and improve public decision-making.

The Technology & Society Institute studies how technological systems develop, acquire institutional support, and become embedded in economic and public life. Its mission is to advance knowledge and inform public decisions through independent research, education, and sustained policy dialogue.

The Institute brings social inquiry into sustained engagement with economic, technical, and institutional analysis. This approach examines how technologies acquire resources and authority, how institutions organize their use, and how their consequences are distributed.

Analytical perspective

Technology, Society, and Institutional Evolution

Scientific knowledge, technological systems, and institutions develop through continuous interaction. New knowledge creates possibilities, while institutions organize the resources, rules, and incentives through which some possibilities are pursued and others are not.

As technologies are incorporated into practice, they can reorganize economic relationships and public authority. Those changes, in turn, alter the conditions under which technologies are developed and governed. The Institute studies these relationships across time, tracing how technological systems gain support, encounter scrutiny, and change through institutional adaptation and practical use.

Institutional principles

01

Research independence

02

Methodological rigor

Methods fit the problem, disclose limitations, and withstand informed scrutiny.

03

Research connects social, economic, and technical analysis according to the problem under study.

04
