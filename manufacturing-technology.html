Research Program 03

Manufacturing Technologies

Production, infrastructure, and industrial systems

Examines how technological knowledge becomes productive capability through manufacturing systems and the institutions that sustain them.

Program focus

Manufacturing determines whether knowledge becomes durable capability.

Scientific advances become economically consequential only when organizations can produce them reliably at scale. Manufacturing therefore links technical design to industrial capability through production systems, infrastructure, and accumulated expertise.

Research examines how these capabilities are built and maintained, and how changes in production reorganize firms, work, and regional economies.

Areas of inquiry

Research priorities

01

Advanced manufacturing

How new production technologies expand what firms can make and alter the organization of manufacturing.

02

Automation and robotics

How automated systems change production practice and redistribute tasks between workers and machines.

03

Semiconductors and critical components

How fabrication capability depends on specialized infrastructure and how geographic concentration shapes industrial resilience.

04

Smart factories

How digital systems integrate production and how data-driven coordination changes factory organization.

05

Supply chains and industrial resilience

How supplier networks create interdependence and how disruption tests the resilience of production systems.

06

Workforce and institutions

How firms and training institutions build technical capability, and how technological change reshapes skill formation.

Guiding questions

  • Which production capabilities are strategically difficult to replace?
  • How should policy balance efficiency, resilience, and regional development?
  • How do automation and industrial AI change work, authority, and skill?
  • Which institutions support reliable advanced manufacturing over time?
