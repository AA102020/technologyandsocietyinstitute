Research Program 04

Military Technologies

Military technology, institutions, and security

Examines how military organizations turn technical systems into operational capability and how new technologies, in turn, reshape military organization and strategy.

Program focus

Military technology acquires value through institutions and force employment.

Technical superiority becomes military capability through organizational adoption and operational practice. The program examines how military institutions adapt to new systems and how those systems alter strategic choices and patterns of conflict.

Research also examines dual-use technologies as they move between commercial and defense settings, tracing how institutional demand and strategic competition shape their development.

Areas of inquiry

Research priorities

01

AI and autonomous systems

How military organizations test and integrate AI and autonomy while maintaining responsibility for consequential decisions.

02

Drones and robotics

How uncrewed systems alter sensing and force employment, and how adversaries adapt through countermeasures.

03

Cyber, space, and communications

How dependence on networked systems changes military resilience and command under contested conditions.

04

Acquisition and defense industry

How procurement institutions connect technological experimentation to production and field adoption.

05

Force design, sustainment, and adaptation

How organizations integrate new capabilities into force structure and sustain them through operational learning.

06

Strategy, ethics, and regulation

How emerging capabilities alter strategic incentives and create new demands for accountability and restraint.

Guiding questions

  • When does technological superiority produce military effectiveness?
  • How do military organizations adapt doctrine and training to new systems?
  • Which controls preserve meaningful human responsibility?
  • How should governments govern dual-use innovation and proliferation?
