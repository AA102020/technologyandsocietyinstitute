Research Program 04

Military Technologies

Military technology, institutions, and security

Examines how acquisition, doctrine, training, logistics, force employment, political control, and organizational learning transform technical systems into military capabilities.

Program focus

Military technology acquires value through institutions and force employment.

Advanced systems affect security through acquisition, doctrine, training, logistics, command structures, organizational learning, and political control. The program examines how military institutions convert technologies into operational capability and how technological change affects deterrence, escalation, civilian protection, and international stability.

Research gives particular attention to dual-use systems and the interaction among commercial innovation, defense demand, industrial policy, export controls, alliances, and strategic competition.

Areas of inquiry

Research priorities

01

AI and autonomous systems

Decision support, autonomy, human control, testing, reliability, targeting, logistics, and operational integration.

02

Drones and robotics

Uncrewed systems, swarms, sensing, countermeasures, adaptation, proliferation, and force employment.

03

Cyber, space, and communications

Network resilience, command and control, electronic warfare, space systems, and contested information environments.

04

Acquisition and defense industry

Procurement, experimentation, test and evaluation, software, commercial technology, production capacity, organizational incentives, and field adoption.

05

Force design, sustainment, and adaptation

Doctrine, training, readiness, logistics, maintenance, mobilization, battlefield adaptation, organizational learning, and integration of new capabilities.

06

Strategy, ethics, and regulation

Deterrence, escalation, arms control, law, accountability, civilian harm, and international stability.

Guiding questions

  • When does technological superiority produce military effectiveness?
  • How do military organizations adapt doctrine and training to new systems?
  • Which controls preserve meaningful human responsibility?
  • How should governments govern dual-use innovation and proliferation?
