Research Program 05

Regulation and Society

Technology governance, expertise, and publics

Examines how laws, standards, classifications, measurements, regulatory institutions, and public contestation shape technological systems, distribute authority, and establish legitimacy.

Program focus

Technology policy organizes innovation, authority, and public protection.

Technology policy includes the laws, regulations, standards, investments, procurement rules, and institutional guidelines that influence how technologies are researched, developed, manufactured, deployed, and reviewed. Effective governance promotes beneficial innovation while protecting privacy, safety, security, fairness, competition, rights, and the public interest.

The program studies regulation as part of technological evolution. Rules shape design and markets, while new technologies alter regulatory capacity, institutional authority, and the meaning of established legal categories.

Areas of inquiry

Research priorities

01

Regulatory design

Risk-based rules, licensing, standards, audits, reporting, liability, enforcement, and adaptive governance.

02

Institutions and oversight

Regulators, legislatures, courts, standards bodies, professional communities, firms, and public-interest organizations.

03

Rights and public values

Privacy, equality, due process, accessibility, autonomy, safety, and democratic accountability.

04

Standards and measurement

Technical standards, benchmarks, classifications, evidence, certification, and infrastructures of compliance.

05

Public participation

Consultation, community knowledge, worker voice, affected groups, contestation, and institutional legitimacy.

06

Technology and state capacity

Digital administration, regulatory technology, procurement, expertise, public infrastructure, and government dependence on private systems.

Guiding questions

  • How do rules influence technological design before systems become entrenched?
  • What evidence makes technology governable and publicly reviewable?
  • Which actors gain authority through standards and technical expertise?
  • How can affected communities participate in consequential technology decisions?
