Research Program 05

Regulation and Society

Technology governance, expertise, and publics

Examines how governance shapes technological development and how technological change, in turn, transforms regulatory institutions and public authority.

Program focus

Technology policy organizes innovation, authority, and public protection.

Governance shapes the conditions under which technologies are developed and used. Institutions translate public purposes into rules and oversight, while technological change continually tests their capacity to respond.

The program studies regulation as part of technological evolution. Rules shape design and markets, while new technologies alter regulatory capacity, institutional authority, and the meaning of established legal categories.

Areas of inquiry

Research priorities

01

Regulatory design

How rules allocate responsibility and adapt as technologies and associated risks change.

02

Institutions, capacity, and oversight

How public institutions acquire the expertise and organizational capacity needed to govern technological systems.

03

Rights and public values

How governance translates public values into protections and how technological systems test established rights.

04

Standards and measurement

How standards and measurement make technological systems comparable, reviewable, and governable.

05

Public participation

How affected groups contest technological choices and how institutions incorporate public knowledge into decisions.

06

Technology and state capacity

How governments use technology to administer public functions and how dependence on technical systems changes institutional capacity.

Guiding questions

  • How do rules influence technological design before systems become entrenched?
  • What evidence makes technology governable and publicly reviewable?
  • Which actors gain authority through standards and technical expertise?
  • How can affected communities participate in consequential technology decisions?
