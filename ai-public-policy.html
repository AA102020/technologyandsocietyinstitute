Research Program 02

AI, Data, and Public Policy

AI, data, and public governance

Examines how algorithms, data infrastructures, organizational practices, expertise, standards, and legal authority shape automated decisions and public accountability.

Program focus

AI policy requires analysis of institutions, markets, and technical systems.

Artificial intelligence systems operate through data, computing infrastructure, organizational practices, labor, procurement, and legal authority. The program examines these arrangements and the consequences of deploying AI in commercial and public settings.

Research addresses safety and performance together with privacy, discrimination, transparency, competition, worker power, public participation, and the capacity of institutions to govern complex systems.

Areas of inquiry

Research priorities

01

AI governance and model risk

Rules, standards, risk classification, validation, documentation, human oversight, liability, monitoring, and institutional accountability.

02

Data privacy and governance

Collection, access, retention, consent, security, data markets, and organizational responsibility.

03

Public-sector AI

Procurement, administrative decisions, public services, benefits, law enforcement, defense, and democratic oversight.

04

Platforms and power

Market concentration, cloud and computing infrastructure, data advantages, labor, and political influence.

05

Automated decisions and evaluation

Validity, reliability, robustness, fairness, explanation, auditing, contestability, review, correction, and consequences for affected people.

06

AI infrastructure and applied analysis

Semiconductors, data centers, energy, supply chains, datasets, computational evaluation, technical labor, and the material foundations of AI.

Guiding questions

  • What evidence should institutions require before deploying high-impact AI?
  • How can regulation make automated decisions reviewable and contestable?
  • How does concentrated technological power affect public authority?
  • Which governance arrangements distribute AI's benefits and risks fairly?
