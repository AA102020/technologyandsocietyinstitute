Research Program 02

AI, Data, and Public Policy

AI, data, and public governance

Examines how institutions, markets, technological systems, and organizational practices shape the development and use of AI, and how AI in turn changes economic activity, public institutions, and social life.

Program focus

AI policy requires analysis of institutions, markets, and technical systems.

Artificial intelligence systems operate through data, computing infrastructure, organizational practices, labor, procurement, and legal authority. The program studies how these arrangements shape AI development, adoption, and use across markets and public institutions.

Research examines how organizations evaluate and govern AI, how data and automated systems affect people and institutions, how markets and industrial structures shape technological development, and how public institutions build and exercise AI capabilities.

Areas of inquiry

Core research domains

01

AI governance and model risk

How institutions classify, evaluate, govern, and monitor AI systems, allocate responsibility, and establish acceptable risk.

02

Data privacy and governance

How institutions collect, access, retain, share, secure, and govern data, and how these arrangements shape privacy, responsibility, markets, and technological capability.

03

Public-sector AI

How public institutions procure, adopt, and use AI, and how these systems reshape administrative capacity, public services, decision-making, accountability, and public authority.

04

AI Markets, Industry, and Institutions

How firms, markets, institutions, infrastructure, data, capital, and labor shape the development of AI, and how control over these resources affects competition, technological capability, and political influence.

Research agenda

Guiding questions

What evidence, evaluation, and oversight should institutions require before deploying consequential AI systems?
How do rules and institutional practices governing data shape privacy, access, responsibility, and technological capability?
Under what conditions does AI strengthen public institutions, and how does its use change administrative capacity, accountability, and public authority?
How do firms, markets, investment, labor, and institutions shape which AI technologies are developed, who can build them, and how their benefits are distributed?
How do compute, semiconductors, data centers, energy, and supply chains constrain and enable AI development and deployment?
How does AI reorganize work, organizations, markets, public institutions, and relationships of authority?

Cross-cutting research

AI Infrastructure

AI development depends on physical, computational, organizational, and industrial systems. The program examines how access to and control over these systems shape technological capability, market structure, institutional dependence, and AI development and deployment.

Compute and semiconductors

How access to chips and computing capacity shapes AI development, entry, competition, and technological capability.

Data centers and energy

How computing facilities, electricity demand, location, and investment connect AI development to energy systems and physical infrastructure.

Cloud and supply chains

How concentrated providers, suppliers, production networks, and infrastructure dependencies shape resilience, access, and institutional capability.

Technical labor and capabilities

How specialized skills, organizational knowledge, and labor markets affect the capacity of firms and public institutions to develop, evaluate, and use AI.

Across the Institute

Connected research

AI intersects with research across the Institute. These connections examine how technological systems develop through economic, industrial, military, regulatory, and institutional settings.

Innovation and Economics

Examines how AI affects innovation, productivity, competition, investment, firms, labor, and technological diffusion, and how economic institutions and market structures influence its development and adoption.

→

Manufacturing Technologies

Examines how semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, production systems, supply chains, industrial capacity, and technical skills shape AI development, and how AI changes manufacturing processes and productive capabilities.

→

Military Technologies

Examines how military organizations develop, acquire, evaluate, and use AI-enabled systems, and how AI interacts with doctrine, training, intelligence, command, logistics, force employment, organizational adaptation, and military capability.

→

Regulation and Society

Examines how laws, standards, classifications, measurements, expertise, and regulatory institutions shape AI development and use, and how AI creates new problems of responsibility, accountability, legitimacy, and public contestation.
