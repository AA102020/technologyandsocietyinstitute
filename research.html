Research agenda

Consequential questions at the intersection of technology and society.

Five programs organize the Institute's research on innovation, governance, industrial capability, security, and institutional change.

01

Innovation and Economics

Innovation, markets, and sociotechnical change

Examines how institutions, infrastructures, investments, labor, knowledge, and public policy shape technological development and distribute its benefits and costs.

AI, Data, and Public Policy

AI, data, and public governance

Examines how algorithms, data infrastructures, organizational practices, expertise, standards, and legal authority shape automated decisions and public accountability.

Manufacturing Technologies

Production, infrastructure, and industrial systems

Examines how technologies, skills, standards, supply chains, organizational practices, and public investment convert scientific knowledge and technical design into productive capability.

Explore → 04

Military Technologies

Military technology, institutions, and security

Examines how acquisition, doctrine, training, logistics, force employment, political control, and organizational learning transform technical systems into military capabilities.

Explore → 05

Regulation and Society

Technology governance, expertise, and publics

Examines how laws, standards, classifications, measurements, regulatory institutions, and public contestation shape technological systems, distribute authority, and establish legitimacy.

Research approach

From technological systems to public consequences.

  1. Define the systemIdentify technologies, institutions, actors, infrastructures, and affected communities.
  2. Trace developmentExamine research, finance, production, deployment, adoption, and contestation.
  3. Assess consequencesEvaluate economic, political, security, labor, rights, and distributional effects.
  4. Clarify governanceCompare regulatory options, institutional responsibilities, and implementation conditions.
